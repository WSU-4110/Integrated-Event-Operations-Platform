== START HERE: BEFORE YOU ARRIVE ==

Do these tonight:
- Verify on Discord. Join the BuilderWars Discord and run /verify. Announcements during the event happen there.
- Find a team (optional). Use /team in #looking-for-team, or come to Team Matchmaking at noon on Saturday. Solo teams are allowed.

- Skim the rules (see the Rules section). Your code has to be written during the 24 hours.

What to bring:
- Laptop and charger (plus a phone charger)
- Your student ID
- Any hardware you plan to build with
- Staying overnight? A sleeping bag or blanket, pillow, toiletries, and a change of clothes
- A water bottle and any medication you need

Leave at home:
- Weapons of any kind, alcohol, or drugs
- Drones, and pets (service animals are welcome)

Doors lock at 8:00 PM Saturday. You can leave anytime, but no one can enter between 8:00 PM and 7:00 AM. If you're staying overnight, be inside by 8:00 PM.

What is BuilderWars?
A hackathon is a building sprint. You and up to three teammates pick a problem, build a working prototype in 24 hours, and demo it to judges. You don't need to be a CS major or have done this before. Mentors from AWS and FREE-WILi are on site the whole time, and most people here are doing their first hackathon.
Your goal: find a real problem, build something that solves it, and show it working. A small project that works beats a big one that doesn't.

Who's hosting:
BuilderWars is run by the AWS Student Builder Group at Wayne State University, a student-led club of 300+ members that runs hands-on cloud workshops, open-source projects, AWS certification study groups, and networking events. Learn more at wayneaws.dev.

== JUDGING ==
Judging is a live, in-person demo at your table. Judges score projects right there, based on what they see working.

The judges:
- Top 3 prizes: a panel of university professors, software engineers, startup founders, and other industry professionals.
- Tracks: representatives from AWS and FREE-WILi, who judge only their own track.

Sunday, step by step:
1. 12:00 PM: submissions close. Eat lunch, then set up your demo at your table.
2. 12:30 PM, three rounds of table demos: teams are split into three groups and judges into three panels. Each round, every panel visits the tables in one group, one team at a time. Panels rotate after each round, so by the end your team has presented to all three panels. You get 5 minutes with each panel to present, demo, and answer questions.
3. Track judging, between your turns: AWS and FREE-WILi judges visit the teams in their tracks while those teams wait for their next panel. If a main panel arrives, track judges step aside until it's done. Stay at your table until organizers release you.
4. After the last round: judges meet to pick the winners.
5. 2:30 PM: winners are announced at the closing ceremony.

What judges look for (every project is scored on the same criteria, whatever the track or theme):
1. Creativity: How original is the idea, and how clever is the approach?
2. Completeness: How much of what you claim actually works? Judges score the live demo, not the slides.
3. Practicality & impact: Would it really solve the problem? Is it a problem many people have?
4. Technical complexity: How hard was it to build, and did you choose tools that fit together, not just buzzwords?

Track judges also look at:
- AWS track: Does AI add something real to a larger app that solves a substantial problem? Do your AWS services fit together naturally? Did you think about integrations and performance tradeoffs? Did you use modern tools creatively, or just name-drop them?
- FREE-WILi track: How deeply and creatively does the project use the FREE-WILi unit? Does the hardware do real work in the project, not just appear in it?

To be judged, you must:
- Submit on Shipyard by 12:00 PM Sunday.
- Have every team member checked in.
- Be at your table with a working demo when your judges arrive.
Judges' decisions are final. Organizers may disqualify projects that break the rules.

== YOUR PITCH ==
You give it three times, once per panel, and each turn is 5 minutes. Present in about 3 to 4 minutes to leave time for questions, and lead with the demo, not slides. Cover:
1. The team. Who you are, your roles, and your experience coming in.
2. The problem. The real-world issue you're solving and who has it.
3. The demo. Show it working. Explain it simply, as if to someone with no background in your topic.
4. How it's built. Your architecture and tech stack, including any AWS services or FREE-WILi features, and how it differs from what already exists.
5. What you learned. The biggest hurdle you hit and how you got past it.

== SUBMITTING ON SHIPYARD ==
Shipyard is where you submit your project and where judges score it. We'll walk through it at the opening ceremony.

What the submission asks for:
- Project title: Your project's name.
- Elevator pitch: One or two sentences: what it does and who it's for.
- Project track: AWS, FREE-WILi, or both. Not entering a track? Choose Other and enter your general theme.
- GitHub repository: Make sure judges can open it (public is easiest). Code should be committed during the 24 hours.
- AI tools used: Every AI tool you used, or "None."
- Demo video (optional): Not required, since judging is live. A 1-2 minute screen recording is a good backup if your live demo breaks, and it's great for your portfolio.

Tip: commit to GitHub often. If your laptop dies Sunday morning, your work is safe.

Steps:
1. Sign up and join BuilderWars at shipyardhq.tech/hackathons/builderwars. Do it tonight or Saturday morning.
2. Set up your team early so the final submission takes two minutes.
3. Submit by 11:30 AM Sunday to leave time for problems. The hard deadline is 12:00 PM.
